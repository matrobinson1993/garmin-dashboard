Tips

  • Listening to YouTube: on a computer, use Chrome or Edge. Open the video in another tab, click Listen to a browser tab, pick that tab and make sure "Also share tab audio" is switched on. Then press play on the video.
  • Keep this page visible: put the two tabs in side-by-side windows so you can watch the tab being written.
  • Recording: tap ● Record in the whole-song section, play the riff (from your guitar or a speaker) close to the phone, tap Stop, then Transcribe song. Recordings are mono, so "Focus on" has no effect on them. A quiet room and the guitar close to the mic help a lot.
  • Best accuracy: use Transcribe a whole song with a short section (the riff you want) set in "Only from … to …". Shorter sections process faster, especially on a phone.
  • Full songs: try Focus on → Guitars – remove centre. It cancels whatever sits in the middle of the stereo mix (usually vocals, bass, kick and snare), leaving guitars panned to the sides. It works on audio files and computer tab listening, not the phone microphone, which only hears one mixed signal. If the guitar is on one side, try Left or Right side only. Results depend on how the song was mixed.
  • Check it by ear: Play back plays the detected notes so you can hear whether they match.
  • Works best on single notes: riffs, melodies and solos. Chords, heavy distortion and busy full-band mixes will give messy results because it follows one note at a time.
  • The white line on the level bar is the trigger point. Notes are only written when the bar goes past it. The app adjusts to your room's background noise after a few seconds.
  • Missing quiet notes? Raise Sensitivity. Getting junk notes? Lower Sensitivity or raise Steadiness.
  • Fast passages: open the song as a file and play it at 50% speed.
  • The string and fret choice is a best guess that keeps your hand in one area of the neck. The real player may have used a different position.
